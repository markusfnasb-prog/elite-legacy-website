## Platform

web

## Stack

Delegated: Next.js + Tailwind CSS + Framer Motion. Reasoning: this is a lead-generation marketing site (quote requests, contact) that needs to support a bold, animation-forward visual world (per the Dribbble inspiration references the user supplied — immersive motion, bold modern layouts) while staying maintainable and easy to extend later (blog, additional forms, possible future gated content). No image-generation tool is available in this environment, so the build proceeds code-first.

## Users

Everyday people searching for life insurance to cover themselves or a loved one, spanning a wide age range (coverage offered from age 0 to 85). This includes parents insuring children, working-age adults insuring themselves or family, and older adults (up to 85) evaluating final-expense/legacy coverage as a serious financial decision. Visitors are not insurance experts; they are comparing options and deciding whether to trust a broker with a significant, personal financial decision.

## Product Purpose

Elite Legacy Insurance Group is an independent life insurance brokerage. The broker works across multiple carriers to help clients find and obtain the right coverage, rather than selling a single insurer's fixed product line. Success on the site means a visitor understands what's possible, trusts the broker, and takes action (requests a quote or makes contact).

## Positioning

Multi-carrier flexibility: the broker is appointed with a range of established carriers — including Mutual of Omaha, Royal Neighbors, TransAmerica, Corebridge Financial, and Americo, among others — and can match a client to the best-fit policy across that range rather than being limited to one company's underwriting and pricing. Coverage spans age 0–85, so the same brokerage serves child policies through late-in-life final-expense/legacy coverage.

## Operating Context

Lead-generation brokerage model: visitors compare coverage options and either request a quote or contact the broker directly (phone or email) to begin the underwriting/application process with one of the partner carriers. The broker is licensed to sell in specific states, not nationwide.

## Product Principles

1. Breadth over a single product pitch: the value is carrier choice and fit, not one policy — messaging and structure should foreground "we shop multiple companies for you," not one insurer's product.
2. Serve the full age spectrum without alienating either end: the same site has to feel relevant to a parent insuring a newborn and a 75-year-old planning final expenses.
3. Credibility is load-bearing: this is a real financial decision, so licensing, real contact info, and (once available) real testimonials matter as much as visual impact.
4. Bold does not mean exclusionary: the user explicitly wants a standout, non-generic design that still reads as easy to navigate for a broad, multi-generational, not-necessarily-tech-savvy audience.
5. Action should stay easy to reach: quote requests and direct contact (phone/email) are the conversion points and should never get buried under visual ambition.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Audience skews toward including older adults (up to 85) making a significant decision, alongside younger, more tech-fluent visitors. The user's direction: aim for both — readable, simply navigable (legible type sizes, clear navigation, easy-to-find phone/email contact) and modern/sleek, rather than trading one for the other.
